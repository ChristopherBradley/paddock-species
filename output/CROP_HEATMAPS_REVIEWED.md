# Paddock-median CFI heatmaps — rebuilt on the hand-reviewed polygons

Regenerated 2026-08-08 by `src/paddocks/crop_heatmap.py --reviewed`, replacing the geometric
quality filter with the reviewer's verdicts. Figures, GeoPackages and row tables are in
`/scratch/xe2/cb8590/paddock-species-data/derived/figures/crop_heatmaps_reviewed/`
(SENSITIVE — TrialCodes and coordinates). The previous version is `CROP_HEATMAPS.md` and its
files are still in `figures/crop_heatmaps/`.

## What changed in the input

| | trials | source of the filter |
|---|---|---|
| before | 2,477 | geometric rules (compactness, area, tree cover) |
| after | **2,301** | 1,973 hand-judged polygons |

The two sets overlap on only **1,929** rows, so this is not a subset in either direction:

- **548 trials the geometric filter kept, the review dropped** — bad polygons.
- **372 trials the geometric filter dropped, the review restored.** These are the
  `compactness > 6` "slivers". The rule was removing 19 % of the data on the theory that long
  thin polygons are drainage lines and road verges, and **the review contradicts it**: 19 of
  the 150 randomly-sampled validation polygons are compactness > 6 and the reviewer passed all
  150. It is off by default under `--reviewed`; `--max-compactness 6` puts it back.

Two flag changes fed this (see `src/paddocks/build_review_package.py`):

- `crop_conflict` now counts **groups, not species**, and is renamed `group_conflict`. A
  polygon shared by a lentil trial and a field pea trial is not a conflict when the target is
  Canola/Cereal/Legume. **120 polygons / 317 trials rescued** (99 all-Legume, 21 all-Cereal);
  50 polygons / 186 trials keep the flag because a canola and a cereal, or a cereal and a
  legume, genuinely share one median.
- All 207 conflicts were checked to be **same-year**, so none is ordinary crop rotation.

## Did cleaning the polygons make the clusters agree with the labels better? Not demonstrably.

Rows are clustered on the CFI trace alone and never see the crop label, so cluster purity is a
label-free check on whether the cleaning bought anything. Raw purity rose:

| | mean over 40 panels |
|---|---|
| purity before | 51.4 % |
| purity after | **54.9 %** (+3.5 pp, 27 panels up / 10 down, Wilcoxon p = 0.005) |

**That number should not be quoted, because the cleaning also changed the class mix.** The
mean majority-class share went 48.6 % → 51.2 %, and purity rises mechanically when one class
grows. Measuring the lift over each panel's own majority-class baseline removes that:

| | mean over 40 panels |
|---|---|
| lift over majority, before | 2.8 pp |
| lift over majority, after | **3.7 pp** (+0.9 pp, 28 up / 12 down, Wilcoxon **p = 0.068**) |

So **+2.6 of the +3.5 pp is class mix**, and what remains is not significant at the 40-panel
scale. The honest statement is that cleaning did not measurably improve cluster–label
agreement; it did not degrade it either. Whether it helps the *model* is a separate and better
-powered question, answered by the five-arm run in `train_group3_reviewed.pbs`.

By state, the residual gain is concentrated where it was always weakest — VIC +9.0 pp raw,
NSW +5.1 — while SA (−0.2) and WA (−0.6) do not move.
