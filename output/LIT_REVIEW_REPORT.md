### Narrative summary

The literature splits cleanly into two tiers relative to this project. The first tier is **global, general-purpose crop-mapping infrastructure** — WorldCereal, Presto, AlphaEarth, Esri/Impact Observatory LULC, and Dynamic World — all of which solve versions of "classify pixels into crop/land-cover classes from Sentinel-1/2 time series at scale," and all of which are candidates either as comparison products or as a foundation to fine-tune. The 2025 wave of papers on foundation-model generalizability and WorldCereal deployment lessons suggests the field is actively grappling with exactly the transfer problem this project poses (global model → local, GRDC-labeled retraining), which validates the brief's research type without yet resolving it for Australia specifically.

The second tier is **Australia-specific precedent**, which is thinner but closer to the target task than expected. Two 2024 papers (WA in-season XGBoost/LSTM; Murray-Darling S1+S2+MODIS fusion) already classify wheat/barley/canola/lupins at regional scale with >90% accuracy — these substantially narrow this project's novelty claim and must be read in full before the experiment-design stage, since the paper's contribution will need to be positioned against them (e.g., continent-wide scale, finer species resolution, or a foundation-model-based methodology none of them use). Separately, CSIRO's own Graincast™ and ePaddocks systems show that the "boundary dataset + yield/crop model" pattern this project wants to replicate has already been operationalized once in Australia, by a group with closely related aims — worth citing and potentially contacting.

The canola-flower-index literature is a clear geographic gap: every yellow-flower spectral index found (Ashourloo's S2 index, the MODIS canola index, at least one competing S2 index) is calibrated on Northern Hemisphere rapeseed phenology. Since the brief already flags canola as the likely first species via this exact mechanism, a Southern Hemisphere recalibration is both low-hanging fruit and a concrete, publishable sub-contribution.

Finally, the GRDC-adjacent Newman & Furbank (2021) NVT dataset is a finding that needs a decision, not just a note: if it substantially overlaps with the project's own NDA-covered GRDC dataset, it could either (a) serve as a public, non-sensitive supplement usable in figures/repro code where the NDA data cannot appear, or (b) already contain much of what this project is trying to assemble, changing the shape of the contribution. This should be checked directly against the trial data before the idea-generation stage locks in a direction.

## Public availability of regional training data (WA and MDB papers) — CONFIRMED 2026-07-24

Both PDFs were supplied by the user (`extra papers/`) and read in full. Neither dataset is publicly downloadable:

- **MDB paper:** crop-type labels (n=193 cereal fields, n=113 canola fields across 6 sites) were "extracted from high-resolution yield maps derived from grain yield monitors mounted on harvesters" — privately-sourced farmer/harvester data. Fig. 1's caption explicitly states "exact field boundaries are not shown to preserve landowners' privacy." The paper itself is open access (CC BY 4.0), but the underlying labelled data is not released. **Confirmed not publicly available.**
- **WA paper:** training labels came from **Digital Agriculture Services (DAS), unpublished data, under a research licence** — and critically, the paper states these "do not represent field-verified ground truth"; they are outputs of DAS's own independently-developed operational crop-mapping system (the same system described in Lawes et al. — i.e., the Graincast/CSIRO lineage already noted in Theme B). A separate, smaller independent test set (340 paddocks, wheat/canola/barley only) came from DPIRD crop-disease surveillance field surveys 2020–2024 — also not a public dataset. **Confirmed not publicly available; and note the important nuance that this paper's "reference labels" are themselves another model's predictions, not independently field-verified crop-type records.**

This last point matters for positioning the project's contribution: unlike both papers, this project's GRDC trial-site labels are genuine field-verified ground truth (real recorded sowings, not another model's output), which is a real methodological edge over training/validating against DAS-derived pseudo-labels — worth stating explicitly in the eventual paper's related-work section.
