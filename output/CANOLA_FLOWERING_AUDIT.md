# Canola paddocks with no flowering signature — why
Generated by `src/paddocks/canola_flowering_audit.py` on the reviewed polygon set (2333 usable trials).
- flowering window DOY 200-300, baseline DOY 100-180, amplitude = peak - baseline
- detection = amplitude above the per-panel 5%-FPR threshold set on that panel's non-canola paddocks
- 614 reviewed-good canola paddocks, of which 9 are unobservable in the flowering window

## The assumption under test: does the field match the trial?

**The window is 4 ha and the median paddock is 57 ha**, so the window is 14x more concentrated on the trial but still contains surrounding field. Contamination can only pull the two series together, so every rate below is a floor, not an estimate.

### Test 1 — threshold crossing (comparable with the headline rate)

The window clears its panel's 5%-FPR threshold and the paddock does not, on **28 of 605 observed canola paddocks (4.6 %)**.

### Test 2 — magnitude against a null (the stronger claim)

On cereal and legume paddocks the trial and its field grow the same crop by construction, so their `window - paddock` amplitude difference is this statistic's null: median +4, 5% tail at **+379** (n=1715, CFI x10000).

- **12 of 605 observed canola paddocks (2.0 %) exceed that cut** — the trial flowered materially more than its field, beyond what same-crop paddocks ever show
- **12 of those also fail paddock-level detection**, which is the defensible count of polygons whose field is probably not canola: 2.0 % of observed canola paddocks
- for reference, canola's own median gap is -144 against the null's +4

### The gap runs the OTHER way, and that is the more useful finding

Canola's median `window - paddock` is **-144**: on the typical canola paddock the FIELD flowers harder than the trial does. Part of that is the estimator and part is not — putting both supports on a mean attenuates it to **-85** against a null of +19, so roughly 29 % of the raw gap was median-versus-mean and the remainder is real spatial structure. The direction survives the control; only its size shrinks.

The agronomy explains it: an NVT canola trial is a mosaic of small variety plots with tracks, buffers and staggered maturities inside a 4 ha window, while the surrounding commercial paddock is one uniform crop flowering together. **The paddock median is not merely a larger sample of the trial's crop — it is a cleaner one.** That is a point in favour of the whole paddock-median design, and it is the reason this test can only be read as a floor.

Character of the threshold-crossing set: median paddock 78 ha against 50 ha for all observed canola; median window amplitude 1502 against paddock 1364.
Upgraded matches: 1/28 here against 3/605 of all observed canola — the review had already dropped 225 of the 259 upgraded matches as `point_outside`, which is the mode the upgrade rule was known to generate.

The complementary case — nothing flowers in the window either — is **116 paddocks**. Those cannot be blamed on the polygon: the trial point itself shows no flowering.

**What they are NOT is failed crops.** `CANOLA_YIELD_CHECK.md` joins these paddocks to the raw NVT yield sheet: every one produced grain, none is flagged `Abandoned`, and their median single-site yield is 1.67 t/ha against 2.55 for detected canola (Mann-Whitney p = 2.6e-12). Canola cannot yield without flowering, so these crops DID flower. The reading is weaker flowering on a poorer crop, not absent flowering — and CFI amplitude tracks canola yield continuously at Spearman rho = 0.38 across all 614 paddocks, so these categories are bins on a gradient rather than distinct failure modes.
