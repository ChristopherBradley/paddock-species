# Predicting yield from the paddock-median series

Written 2026-08-09. Generated by `train_yield.py` / `train_yield.pbs`; per-arm tables in
`YIELD_optical.md` (widest coverage), `YIELD_optical_ctl.md` and `YIELD_optical_s1.md` (the
matched pair). Follows the lead in `CANOLA_YIELD_CHECK.md`, where CFI flowering amplitude was
found to track canola yield at Spearman 0.38.

---

## 1. The result

**Spatial transfer — 5-fold GroupKFold on site, identical rows in all three arms.** This is the
split that matches the actual product question: predict a paddock nobody has visited.

| crop | n | year+state baseline R² | optical R² | **optical + S1 R²** | RMSE t/ha (best) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Canola** | 571 | 0.290 | 0.271 | **0.371** | 0.70 |
| **Wheat** | 791 | 0.325 | 0.583 | **0.603** | 1.14 |
| **Barley** | 220 | 0.089 | 0.542 | 0.529 | 1.10 |

**Temporal transfer — train ≤2022, predict 2023-24, same rows:**

| crop | n | year+state baseline R² | optical R² | **optical + S1 R²** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canola | 144 | −0.282 | 0.083 | **0.212** |
| Wheat | 169 | −0.650 | 0.437 | **0.506** |
| Barley | 69 | −0.389 | **0.539** | 0.516 |

**Yield is predictable from the paddock series for all three crops.** Wheat and barley reach
R² ≈ 0.54-0.60 spatially and ≈ 0.51-0.54 temporally, roughly doubling what year and state alone
can do. Spearman rho is 0.73-0.79 for the cereals.

## 2. The finding worth carrying: canola yield needs Sentinel-1, canola *identity* does not

**Canola spatial R² goes 0.271 → 0.371 when S1 is added — the largest single-feature effect in
this table.** Without backscatter, the satellite features do not beat knowing the year and the
state (0.271 against a 0.290 baseline). With it they beat that baseline by +0.081. Temporally
the same story: 0.083 → 0.212.

Cereals barely move (wheat +0.020, barley −0.013).

**This is the exact inverse of the classification result.** In `S1_MODEL.md`, S1 did nothing for
binary canola (0.927 → 0.934, detection rate moving the wrong way) and everything for pulses.
Here it does nothing for cereals and everything for canola.

The mechanism is coherent. Canola's *identity* is written in flowering colour, which CFI already
reads to near-saturation — so there is nothing left for S1 to add. Canola's *yield* is a
function of biomass and canopy structure, which is exactly what backscatter measures and
exactly what a flowering index cannot see once the canopy is yellow. `CANOLA_YIELD_CHECK.md`
found CFI amplitude tracks canola yield at Spearman 0.38: real, but a ceiling. S1 is what
completes it — Spearman goes to 0.625.

**Planning consequence.** Dropping S1 makes the canola *map* cheaper at no accuracy cost, but
it removes canola *yield* entirely. Those two products have opposite requirements and should be
costed separately — see `NATIONAL_INFERENCE_BENCHMARK.md` §3, where S1 at national scale is a
12-day copyq problem.

## 3. Why the baseline is negative on temporal transfer, and why that is honest

The year+state baseline scores R² −0.28 to −0.65 on the temporal split — worse than predicting
a constant. That is not a bug. Training stops at 2022, so the 2023 and 2024 year-dummies are
all-zero in training and the model has no way to know those seasons; it extrapolates the wrong
year effect and is punished for it.

**That is the real operational situation** — you cannot know a season's effect before the
season — which is why it is the right bar. It is also a *weak* bar, and the +1.0 R² "gains"
against it on the temporal split should not be quoted as if the satellite features explained
all of that. The spatial comparison, where the baseline is a healthy 0.09-0.33, is the fair one.

## 4. What the target actually is — the limit on any yield map built from this

**NVT single-site yield is a replicated variety-trial yield under trial management, averaged
over ~17 varieties. It is not commercial paddock yield.** Trial plots generally out-yield
commercial crops.

The label itself is precise — within-trial variety sd is 0.28 t/ha, so the trial mean has a
standard error near 0.07 t/ha. The uncertainty is entirely in the transfer from trial to
paddock, and `CANOLA_FLOWERING_AUDIT.md` shows that transfer is not even reliably
one-directional: the surrounding field flowers *harder* than the trial (median window−paddock
CFI amplitude −144), so the paddock is not simply a degraded copy of the trial.

**Anything shipped from this predicts NVT-equivalent yield and inherits an unquantified
offset.** Stating that plainly is better than calibrating it away with a constant nobody
measured. Quantifying it would need commercial yield data — grower records or a yield-map
dataset — which this project does not have.

Scale check on the error: RMSE 0.70 t/ha against a 2.38 t/ha canola median is 29 %; 1.14
against 3.92 for wheat is 29 %; 1.10 against 4.18 for barley is 26 %. Consistent, and large
enough that per-paddock numbers should always ship with an interval rather than alone.

## 5. Caveats

- **Barley rests on 220 trials and 69 temporal test rows.** Its spatial baseline (0.089) is the
  weakest of the three, which inflates its apparent gain (+0.45). Treat the barley numbers as
  indicative.
- **No hyper-parameter tuning.** `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` at defaults, one fit per arm.
  The classifier work established these fits are deterministic below 10,000 rows, so there is
  no seed variance to report — but there is no tuning headroom explored either.
- **Geography excluded from the feature arms**, as in the classifier, so these numbers describe
  what the imagery carries rather than where the good country is. The `satellite + year/state`
  rows in the per-arm files show what adding it back buys (typically +0.02-0.04).
- **Group, not species.** At inference the classifier emits Cereal, not Wheat-or-Barley, and
  wheat and barley have different yield distributions (median 3.92 vs 4.18 t/ha). A deployed
  cereal yield layer needs either a pooled cereal model or a species split the classifier
  cannot currently supply — `GROUP3_MODEL.md` records that barley→wheat was the dominant
  confusion in the 9-class model. **This is unresolved and blocks a cereal yield map.**
