# 4. Methodology

Figure 1 summarizes the pipeline end-to-end: Sentinel-2 time series drive both paddock segmentation and a spectral-index classifier; a presence gate determines whether a segmented paddock is classified at all; and a calibrated model attributes Cereal yield to classified paddocks. This section describes each stage; Section 5 reports the accuracy and validation results the design choices below were selected against.

## 4.1 Segmentation

Each tile was segmented with the Segment Anything Model (`vit_h` checkpoint, stock parameters; Kirillov et al., 2023), applied via the SAMGeo/PaddockTS wrapper. Re-tuning SAM's own segmentation parameters (point-grid density, IoU and stability-score thresholds) was investigated as a route to reducing large, under-segmented "blob" polygons — SAM's single largest failure mode in this application, discussed in Section 7 — and was ruled out: parameter sweeps did not resolve the failure mode at a cost proportionate to the benefit, and the area/compactness mask described below (Section 3.4) was retained as the primary control instead. Resulting polygons were filtered to a 5-300 ha area range with a compactness ratio of at most 8, a purely geometric, non-spectral signal that functions as a first, free presence indicator ahead of any spectral classification.

## 4.2 Labelling

GRDC National Variety Trial (NVT) points were matched to their enclosing (or, where no polygon contained the point, nearest plausible) SAM polygon. Two label-integrity problems were addressed explicitly rather than left implicit in the training data. First, because NVT trials are agronomic variety comparisons, multiple trials of different crops are frequently co-located within the same physical paddock (69.7% of training trials share a segmented paddock with at least one other trial; 31.8% of those co-located pairs are of different crops), which would otherwise inject direct label noise into paddock-level features. Collapsing the original nine-species scheme to three broad groups (Canola; Cereal, comprising wheat, barley, and oat; Legume, comprising chickpea, faba bean, field pea, lentil, and lupin) resolves 69% of these conflicts by construction, because co-located trials of different species within the same broad group no longer conflict. The remaining ambiguous matches were resolved by hand review of a stratified sample of flagged polygons, which measurably improved classifier performance on canola detection and on temporal transfer (Section 5.1) relative to a size-matched, unreviewed control. Second, a small number of matches to implausibly small polygons (a match falling within a road or laneway sliver rather than the adjacent real paddock) were upgraded to a nearby, at least three times larger polygon within 150 m of the trial point, following a matcher rule shared identically between the training-label-extraction and human-review pipelines so that the two cannot drift apart.

## 4.3 Classification

A `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` was trained on the 51 paddock-median, day-of-year-binned spectral features described in Section 3.4, to predict one of the three crop groups. Because a crop map intended for repeated, multi-season use must generalize both across time (future seasons the model was not trained on) and across space (paddocks and regions the model was not trained on), the classifier was evaluated under two distinct held-out protocols rather than a single train/test split: a temporal-transfer split (training on trials sown 2022 or earlier, testing on 543 fixed trials sown 2023-2024) and a spatial-transfer split (5-fold `GroupKFold` cross-validation grouped by trial site, scored on the same fixed 543-trial test set). Using the identical fixed test set for both splits ensures the two numbers answer genuinely different questions — "does it generalize across time" versus "does it generalize across new locations" — rather than differing because of which rows happen to be scorable under each protocol, a confound identified and corrected during development (an earlier, incorrect comparison scored each arm only on the rows its own label-cleaning step retained, which produced a spuriously large apparent gain from label review alone; see Section 5.1).

## 4.4 Presence gating

Because the classifier is trained only on trial points known a priori to be one of the three crop groups, it has no mechanism to recognize a segmented polygon that is not cropped at all — pasture, native vegetation, urban land, or any other non-target land use that happens to pass the geometric area/compactness filter. A presence gate is therefore applied ahead of classification to decide whether a segmented, geometrically plausible polygon is classified at all, or is instead retained with an explicit abstain reason.

The shipped presence gate uses NDVI seasonal amplitude (the difference between the 90th- and 10th-percentile NDVI values across the growing season) with a threshold of 0.35, fitted using presence-only optimization against 3,439 NVT trial paddocks — the only population for which "this location is definitely cropped" ground truth exists; no comparably field-verified population of definitely-non-cropped land was available to fit a genuine presence/absence classifier against. An earlier design considered an absence-labelled negative class built from presence-only farmer-recorded grazing records (a retired dataset, AgriWebb); this was abandoned after finding that 78.5% of a "hard stratum" of grazing paddocks passed the same crop-presence test as known crop paddocks, indicating the negative labels themselves were not reliably negative (Section 6.4).

As a more theoretically principled alternative to amplitude alone, we designed and tested a phenology-shape presence gate requiring both a green-up rise and a post-harvest senescence drop in the seasonal NDVI curve, in two variants: an oracle version anchored to each trial's recorded sowing and harvest dates, and a deployable version anchored instead to the detected seasonal NDVI peak (since sowing/harvest dates are not available at inference time outside the trial network). Both variants were evaluated first for held-out presence recall against the same fixed test population as the amplitude gate, and then — critically, because presence recall alone cannot answer whether a stricter gate actually improves the map's real area-inflation problem — for their effect on the regional (100 km Riverina) area ratio against ABS sown-area statistics, across the full nine-year regional dataset (Section 5.4).

## 4.5 Yield estimation

A `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`, using the identical 51-feature contract as the classifier, was trained to predict Cereal yield (wheat, barley, and oat pooled to match the classifier's own Cereal group) for classified Cereal polygons. Canola yield was not shipped: an optical-only model's coefficient of determination underperformed a trivial year-and-state-mean baseline, and shipping a yield estimate that does worse than guessing the year and region would be misleading rather than useful (Section 7, Limitation 5). Because NVT trial yields are agronomic-trial yields, not commercial-paddock yields, and are known to differ systematically, the Cereal yield model's raw output was calibrated to ABS state-level yield statistics with a single national multiplicative factor (0.6248), fit on one year of ABS data and checked, out of sample, against two further held-out years. Both the raw (NVT-trial-equivalent) and the ABS-calibrated yield columns are shipped on every classified Cereal polygon; only shipping the calibrated column would hide the size and direction of the calibration adjustment from a downstream user.

## 4.6 Validation protocol

The national map is validated against ABS sown-area statistics aggregated to the SA2 level, restricted to the subset of SA2s where the mapped tile footprint covers at least 50% of the SA2's area — a threshold that turns the comparison from a spatial sample of an SA2's crop mix (which paddock-mix spatial clustering can bias) into something closer to a census of it. Two complementary comparisons are made: a scale-free composition comparison (does the map's crop-type *share*, e.g. the fraction of mapped crop area that is Canola, match ABS's share), and, for the subset of well-covered SA2s where the mapped footprint's absolute area is meaningful, an area-ratio comparison (does the map call more or less land "crop-present" than ABS records as sown). This composition/area distinction is what allows the results in Section 5.2 to separate a presence-detection failure (an area-ratio problem) from a classification failure (a composition problem) rather than conflating the two into a single accuracy number. As a validation-design decision, we explicitly did not attempt an Olofsson-style (Olofsson et al., 2014) stratified-probability-sample area estimation with confidence intervals, because no probability sample of field-verified crop-species labels exists for Australia at the density this would require (Section 2.4, Section 7).

A separate diagnostic — comparing each polygon's argmax-predicted class against its mean predicted-probability class — was used to test whether an observed classification disagreement (Section 5.2, the Legume over-prediction) could be explained by a decision-rule artefact (e.g., systematic near-ties broken one way by argmax) rather than a genuine classification error; the two agree, ruling out the cheap explanation.

Independently of the ABS comparison, we compared the national map spatially, at the pixel level, against two products with no connection to this project's training or validation pipeline: WorldCereal 2021 v100 (Van Tricht et al., 2023) and NLUM v7 250 m per-commodity probability surfaces (2020-21). Because WorldCereal has no Canola or Legume class, this comparison is restricted to (a) crop presence/absence, checked against WorldCereal's `temporarycrops` layer, and (b) the Cereal class, checked against WorldCereal's `wintercereals` layer (WorldCereal's `springcereals` layer was confirmed, not assumed, to have zero coverage over the Australian bounding box, consistent with Australia's cereal season falling entirely under WorldCereal's `wintercereals` calendar convention). NLUM, by contrast, provides a direct per-commodity probability surface for all three of our target groups (oilseed, cereal, legume, each combining winter and summer seasonal variants by taking the maximum probability), plus a grazing-probability layer used to test whether abstained (unclassified) polygons preferentially fall in high-grazing-probability land. Because NLUM's commodity layers are not calibrated to comparable magnitudes against each other (its cereal layer runs substantially higher everywhere than its oilseed or legume layers), comparisons across commodities use an enrichment ratio — the median commodity probability at points of a given predicted class, divided by the median at abstained points, for the same commodity layer — rather than raw probability magnitudes, to avoid a cross-commodity scale artefact. Both comparisons were run on a stratified random sample of 250,000 of the 1,346,582 national paddock centroids (18.6%); because these comparisons measure proportions and agreement rates rather than absolute area totals, a sample is statistically adequate, unlike the ABS area-ratio comparison, which specifically requires a census-like footprint to be meaningful (Section 4.6, first paragraph).

## 4.7 Multi-year regional generalization check

The identical pipeline (segmentation through classification and presence gating) was re-run across nine seasons (2017-2025) over the 100 km Riverina regional block, used for two purposes: a purely geometric check of polygon stability across years (independent of any spectral classification, since a real paddock's boundary should be roughly stable year to year — median IoU 0.84, with the polygon found in a median of 7 of 9 years), and a year-by-year check of classified-share and mean NDVI-amplitude behaviour, used to identify seasons where the pipeline behaves structurally differently (Section 5.6). Extending this multi-year re-run to the full national extent is planned but has not yet been executed at the time of this draft [PENDING: national 2017-2025 run, E8]; all multi-year results in this paper are from the 100 km regional extent only.
